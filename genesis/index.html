Genesis / cyberpunk romantasy

Her family stole the truth.
His mother was the first thing they erased.

In a near-future Singapore and Bangkok, grief can be edited, truth can be managed, and power wears the language of mercy. Genesis is a dark, addictive cyberpunk romantasy trilogy about memory, class, desire, and the cost of choosing what is real.

The premise

The future learned to sell relief.

Genesis starts inside a private house where family power hides behind taste, money, and manners. The technology is intimate: grief can be softened, shame can be managed, and inconvenient memory can be cleaned until obedience feels like healing.

May has inherited a life built on careful omissions. Kit has survived by remembering what everyone else was paid to forget. The danger is not that the city lies. It is that the lie works.

The system does not erase people. It improves them until nobody can find the original.

For readers who want the noir atmosphere of Blade Runner with the emotional stakes of romantasy: this is what happens when surveillance meets desire, and the only person who remembers is the one you're not supposed to love. Cyberpunk gives Genesis its surveillance, scale, and class violence. Romantasy gives it the ache: two people trying to stay real when every institution around them has learned how to edit pain. As a cyberpunk romantasy, the trilogy refuses to let the romance be a subplot — it is the evidence of the crime.

The world

Singapore writes the rules. Bangkok finds the cracks.

The surface is luxury: towers, clinics, family offices, rooms that know how to keep secrets. Underneath, the same machinery runs through repair markets, safehouses, debt ledgers, and memory work sold as care.

The trilogy shape

Three books. One widening crime.

Each book widens the investigation without letting go of the core romance: May and Kit trying to stay real in a world where truth has become an expensive preference.

Book I

The House That Remembers

May enters a family estate where every room has been arranged to make violence look like inheritance. Kit knows the house too well, and what he remembers turns their attraction into a liability. The mystery begins as a private wound, then opens onto the first proof that memory itself is being managed.

Closed-room romance / inheritance horror

Book II

The City of Cruel Mercies

The secret leaves the house and enters the city. Clinics, creditors, family offices, and street-level fixers all touch the same hidden system: mercy sold as control. May and Kit follow the trail through Singapore power and Bangkok improvisation while their relationship becomes something the city can use against them.

City thriller / desire under surveillance

Book III

The World We Kept

By the final book, exposure is not enough. The old order is already preparing a cleaner story about itself, and the people it edited are still carrying the damage. May and Kit have to decide what survives: revenge, repair, or a version of love honest enough to build from.

What is Genesis?

Genesis is a cyberpunk romantasy trilogy set in near-future Singapore and Bangkok. It follows May Tan, a guarded heiress, and Kit, a man who remembers what her family helped erase.

Is it romantasy or cyberpunk?

Both. Cyberpunk gives Genesis its surveillance, memory technology, class violence, and city scale. Romantasy gives it the central emotional engine: May and Kit's dangerous relationship drives the plot.

Where is it set?

Near-future Singapore and Bangkok: polished family offices, private clinics, repair markets, safehouses, debt systems, and memory work sold as care.

Who are May and Kit?

May is a guarded heiress raised inside a family system built on omissions. Kit is the person who remembers what others were paid to forget. Their romance makes memory dangerous.
