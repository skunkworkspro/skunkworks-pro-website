A SONG FOR THREE BODIES
Wong Kar Wai-influenced Beat Sheet
105 pages | Thai-language feature | Bangkok

Core logline:
A sound-collector who translates everyone's feelings instead of feeling her own drags her domestic boyfriend and returning ex-lover into a messy triad — and what breaks isn't who loves more, but who can stop performing first.

Thematic question:
Can you be loved as you are, or must you always be someone interesting first?

Style rules:
- Handheld, close, sweaty. Bangkok heat sticks to skin.
- Characters in motion — walking, eating, riding motorbikes at night. They never sit still when something matters.
- Time is fluid: voiceover floats over images of empty rooms, half-eaten food, rain on windows.
- Pop song mutates across acts. One song — same recording — reveals new meaning each time.
- Strange objects return: a landlady's written house rules taped to the wall, a broken ceiling fan that only works on speed 3, a jar of fermented fish in the corner that nobody dares touch.
- Lulu's sound file labels are poems — never say what she means out loud.

SONG MOTIF: "สุดท้ายแล้ว" (In the End, After All) — a fictional cheap 90s Thai luk thung pop ballad by a forgotten singer. Tinny, over-produced, dripping with melodrama. Lulu found it pirated onto a CD-R at the record shop. It plays in full three times: once as mockery, once as surrender, once as truth.

ACT ONE — The Safety Room (pp.1-25)

1. OPENING IMAGE (p.1)
[Rehearsal room, late afternoon]
Extreme close-up: a microphone diaphragm. Lulu's breath. She records: "KREUNG-01 — the sound of a ceiling fan that can only spin on speed three." A Thai pop song bleeds through the wall from the next room. She taps her pen. Jiro texts: "Fish sauce almost empty. Pick up?" She saves the file. Does not answer.

2. THEME STATED (pp.2-4)
[Record shop, evening]
Lulu at work rearranging the 'orphaned CDs' section. A customer — old woman — asks for a song she can't remember the name of, only the feeling. Lulu hums it back to her. Old woman: "You understand people better than they understand themselves. Must be exhausting." Lulu laughs. Does not hear it.

3. SET-UP — Lulu and Jiro's world (pp.4-10)

a) [Jiro's apartment, morning]
The apartment has rules. The landlady taped them to the kitchen wall nine years ago:
- No durian in the fridge (RULE 1)
- No guests after midnight (RULE 2)
- If the fan goes to speed 4, unplug it immediately and call the building manager (RULE 3)
- "The plants are not to be watered with anything but yesterday's rice water" (RULE 7)

Jiro waters the plants with yesterday's rice water. He has breakfast ready. Lulu eats without looking up, editing an influencer's captions on her phone. Their silence is full — the kind of comfortable that is also a kind of hiding.

b) [BTS Skytrain, evening]
Lulu wears headphones. She free-assigns sound files: "CITY-47 — the hum between Siam and Chit Lom, like a held breath." She watches a couple argue silently across the carriage. She names them in her head: The Man Who Always Apologizes, The Woman Who Stopped Believing It.

c) [Jiro's kitchen, night]
Jiro making pad grapao. He asks Lulu about her song. She says it's not ready. He says he believes her. This is his flaw: he always believes her. She kisses his neck to change the subject. He lets her.

4. CATALYST (pp.11-12)
[Cheap rehearsal room, afternoon]
Lulu arrives to teach a vocal coaching session. The room is booked to a 'Saranrat T.' — Lulu's former student list doesn't have that name. Through the door: a woman singing a warm-up badly on purpose. Lulu opens the door.

Sara. Back from Berlin. Older. Sharper. Smiling like she knew this would happen.

Sara: "I need someone to teach me to sound like I'm not trying. You were always good at that."

Lulu's voiceover: "I saved that session as SARA-01 — return of a signal I thought I'd permanently deleted. Do not reply."

5. DEBATE (pp.13-25)

a) [Jiro's apartment, night]
Lulu is quiet at dinner. Jiro notices. She says she ran into an old friend from Berlin. His face doesn't change but his hands pause mid-serve. He adds extra chili to her plate — his way of saying "I see you." She doesn't notice.

b) [Café, day]
Lulu meets Sara for coffee. Sara is building her "trying to become normal" brand — a clean-looking rebrand from years of monetized performance in Europe. She shows Lulu her content calendar: "Day 1: buying vegetables without makeup. Day 2: failing to fold laundry. Day 3: crying at a bus stop — real or staged is the tension, isn't it?"

Lulu laughs. First time in months.

Sara, offhand: "You used to write songs. Did you stop, or just hide them in folders?"

c) [Jiro's apartment, late night]
Jiro asleep. Lulu opens a laptop. Scrolls through old sound files. Finds BERLIN-12 — footsteps on a wet street, someone laughing. She plays it. Holds her breath. Closes it.

d) [Sara's rented room, night]
Sara alone. She tapes a piece of paper to her mirror: "RULE FOR MYSELF: don't perform. Not even when you're alone." She rips it off. Tapes it again.

e) [Motorbike taxi, night]
Lulu rides through Bangkok at speed. Her voiceover: "The city at night sounds like a song you can almost remember. I keep trying to record it. But the mic picks up traffic instead of longing."

f) [Market, morning]
Jiro buys fish. The vendor asks when he and Lulu will get married. He smiles. Doesn't answer. Buys extra fish sauce anyway.

g) [Rehearsal room, afternoon]
Sara's first coaching session. Lulu teaches her to breathe from the diaphragm. Sara sings badly on purpose. Lulu corrects her. Sara: "You always did like fixing me." Close of their faces. Not a kiss, but more than a session.

BREAK INTO TWO (p.25)
[Alley behind the rehearsal room, dusk]
Sara lights a cigarette. Lulu has never smoked. Sara offers. Rain starts. Lulu takes the cigarette. They share it under a corrugated overhang. Sara's hand brushes Lulu's. Neither pulls away.

Lulu's voiceover: "I named that moment UNDER-OVER-01. The sound of rain on rust, plus somebody else's heartbeat through wet fabric. Category: unreleased."

ACT TWO A — The Trying-On (pp.25-52)

6. B STORY — Sara's world (pp.26-30)

a) [Sara's room, afternoon]
Sara filming content: "Day 14 — I bought a plant today. I'm going to try to keep it alive." She looks at the camera. For one second, real exhaustion breaks through the performance. She cuts the take. Starts over.

b) [Jiro's office, day]
Jiro works for a small food-export company. A colleague asks if he and Lulu are serious. Jiro: "I don't know if serious is the right word. We're just… doing time together happily." He says it like a question.

c) [Record shop, evening]
Lulu finds the CD-R. "สุดท้ายแล้ว" — In the End, After All. She plays it on the shop's cheap speakers. A 90s Thai pop ballad: synths, overdone vibrato, lyrics about waiting for someone who never comes. She laughs at it. But doesn't put it down.

7. FUN AND GAMES — The affair (pp.30-52)

a) [Rehearsal room, various]
A montage of vocal sessions that are really seduction:
- Sara singing scales. Lulu's hand on her diaphragm to correct posture. Doesn't move.
- Sara saying a lyric to Lulu's face instead of singing it.
- Lulu recording: "SARA-04 — the way she breathes before she lies. Useful reference."
- Falling. Literally. Sara trips on a cable. Lulu catches her. They laugh. Too long. Too close.

b) [Street food stall, night]
Jiro and Lulu eating. Jiro mentions a coworker's wedding. Lulu's phone buzzes — Sara. She turns it face-down. Jiro sees. Says nothing.

c) [Hotel room, afternoon]
First sex with Sara. The scene is: intimate, handheld, close on skin but not explicit. What matters: Sara stops. "What are we doing?" Lulu: "Not thinking." Sara: "That's the problem."

Afterward, Lulu records: "HOTEL-12 — the sound of someone else's AC unit, and the silence of a person who just told you the truth and you ignored it."

d) [Jiro's apartment, night]
Jiro has made Lulu's favorite — gaeng hung lay. She barely eats. He watches her push food around the plate. His voice: "Do you want to tell me something?" Her: "I'm tired." He nods. Washes the dishes. The choreography of avoidance.

e) [Sara's room, night]
Sara filming: "Day 22 — I'm not sure what I'm doing but it's not branding anymore." She doesn't post it.

f) [Jiro's apartment, late night — THE CONFRONTATION]
Jiro is awake when Lulu comes home at 2am. She smells different — not perfume, just different. He doesn't ask. He says: "I found something on your laptop. SARA-04."

Long silence.

Jiro, quietly: "I don't think you're doing this to hurt me. I think you're doing this because you don't know how to want something without making it a secret first."

Lulu: "I'm sorry."

Jiro: "I don't want sorry. I want to know what you actually want."

She can't answer.

Jiro, after a long pause: "What if we all had dinner?"

8. MIDPOINT (pp.52-55) — The throuple dinner

[Restaurant on the river, night — but cheap, plastic chairs, one light that flickers]

The three of them. Jiro ordered for everyone — he knows what's good. Sara is disarmed by the gesture. They talk. It's not awkward, which is more unsettling than if it were.

Sara, to Jiro: "She used to leave voice notes in German when she didn't want me to understand. I learned German."

Jiro, to Sara: "She records everything. Once she recorded me sleeping. Called it JIRO-09 — the sound of someone who trusts you. I don't think she meant it as a compliment but I took it as one."

Lulu: "I'm right here. You can talk to me."

Jiro: "We are talking about you. That's different."

Sara laughs. Jiro smiles. Something true passes between them — a brief, awful alliance of people who love the same impossible person.

Midpoint reversal: Lulu realizes she is not the center of the thing she started. She's the space between them. And for the first time, that frightens her more than being chosen.

ACT TWO B — The Three-Body Problem (pp.56-82)

9. FUN AND GAMES CONTINUED — The triad works, briefly (pp.56-70)

a) [Sunday market, day]
The three of them. Jiro picks fruit. Sara films him without asking. Jiro: "What's the caption?" Sara: "Man who picks the best mangoes. How to caption sincerity?" He laughs. Real.

b) [Jiro's apartment — first throuple night, night]
Landlady's rules broken (RULE 2: no guests after midnight). The three of them on the floor. Jiro plays "สุดท้ายแล้ว" on his phone. Sara: "What IS this song?" Lulu: "A joke I found at work." Sara: "It's terrible." They sing it badly together. Laughing. Bodies close.

The intimacy that follows: not pornographic. What matters is the morning — Jiro making breakfast for two people. Sara watching him. The quiet domesticity of it. For one moment, all three of them look like they might survive this.

c) [Various — montage of the triad trying]
- All three buying a couch together (they can't agree, buy none, but the arguing is flirtatious).
- Lulu recording: "LOVE-01 — the sound of three people not knowing what to do with their hands."
- Sara teaching Jiro to dance badly in the living room. Lulu watches. She's happy. Then a flicker of something else — the thing she doesn't name.
- A cooking disaster that ends with them ordering food, still laughing.

d) [Sara and Jiro alone, night]
Jiro walks Sara to the bus stop. It's raining. They wait under a shelter.

Sara: "Why did you agree to this?"

Jiro: "Because she needs something I can't give her. And maybe I need something she can't give me."

Sara: "And what's that?"

Jiro: "Someone who sees me as a choice, not a default."

This is the moment the B-story leg becomes real. Sara sees him. He sees her seeing him.

Bus arrives. Sara doesn't get on. "One more bus." They stay in the rain.

10. BAD GUYS CLOSE IN — The crack (pp.70-78)

a) [Record shop, day]
Lulu finds an old CD — a Berlin band she and Sara loved. She texts Sara: "Found our song." Sara replies 4 hours later: "Sorry was at lunch with Jiro — he showed me how to make the noodles you hate."

Lulu stares at the phone. The crack.

b) [Jiro's apartment, night]
Lulu comes home. Jiro is stir-frying. Sara is sitting at the table, drinking tea, like she lives there. The domestic ease between them hits Lulu like a physical thing.

Lulu: "Did you know she was coming?"

Jiro: "I invited her. I thought it would be nice."

Lulu: "You should have asked me."

Sara: "You're the one who doesn't ask anyone anything."

First real fight. Not about sex. About who gets to decide what family looks like.

c) [Café collaboration prep — the disaster, day]
The three of them plan a small live-streamed event. Sara's brand. Jiro's food. Lulu's music. The premise: "Normal People Doing Normal Things."

It goes wrong. Sara catches a crew member filming a setup shot — Jiro peeling garlic — that she didn't direct. She snaps. The performance cracks. Jiro walks off. Lulu is left holding the camera that's still recording.

Sara, later, to Lulu: "I can't do the 'real' thing. I spent too long being fake on purpose. I don't know how to turn it off."

Lulu: "Then turn off the camera."

Sara: "If I turn off the camera, what's left of me?"

d) [Jiro's apartment, late night]
Jiro and Lulu in bed. Not touching.

Jiro: "I think I'm in love with her too."

Lulu: "That's not how this works."

Jiro: "You can't start a triangle and then get angry when it becomes three-sided."

She has no answer.

11. ALL IS LOST (pp.78-82)
[Café collab live event, night]

The stream goes out anyway. Sara performs — but it's not her brand. It's raw. She talks about the escort years. The room goes silent. Jiro watches from the kitchen door. Lulu watches from behind the camera.

Sara: "I thought if I could be a normal person I would be worth loving. But I was worth loving even when I was getting paid, and I didn't believe it then either. So what was the point?"

Someone posts the clip. It does not go truly viral — but it circulates far enough to humiliate her in exactly the wrong rooms.

Whiff of Death: Sara's past is now legible to the wrong people. Not a blackmail plot — just the consequence of authentic performance in a world that punishes real vulnerability.

Sara runs. Jiro doesn't follow. Lulu can't move.

ACT THREE — The Solo Recording (pp.82-105)

12. DARK NIGHT OF THE SOUL (pp.82-90)

a) [Jiro's apartment, next day — empty]
Lulu wakes to find Jiro's side of the closet empty. He left a note. Not dramatic. A shopping list. "Fish sauce. Eggs. Rice. The plants need the rice water — please don't forget."

She reads it twelve times. The care in the list. The absence.

b) [Sara's room, day — shuttered]
Sara has pulled all her content. In the dark. Her phone won't stop buzzing. She turns it off. Watches the ceiling.

c) [Various — Lulu wandering]
Fragments. She rides the BTS. Records: "CITY-91 — the sound of nobody waiting for you at the end of the line."

She stands outside the rehearsal room. Doesn't go in.

She listens to "สุดท้ายแล้ว" on headphones. Doesn't laugh this time.

d) [Jiro at his office, day]
He's at his desk. Normal. His coworker asks if he's okay. He says he quit his relationship. She doesn't know what to say to that. Neither does he.

e) [Sara alone, night]
She watches her old Instagram stories — the ones she hasn't deleted yet. A year ago. Berlin. A hotel room. A life she spent so long curating. She sees the loneliness in it that she tried to edit out.

f) [Lulu at the record shop, closing time]
She finds the CD-R again. Puts it in the shop player. Listens to the whole thing, alone. The terrible synth. The melodramatic lyrics. The voice of someone who actually meant it, even if it was cheap and overdone.

Lulu's voiceover: "I always thought real feeling had to be perfect. But maybe it just has to be real. Even if it sounds like this."

13. BREAK INTO THREE (pp.90-92)
[Rehearsal room, night — Lulu alone]

She opens the room. Sits at the keyboard. Presses record.

For the first time, she doesn't name the file. She just plays.

The song starts. It's not finished. It's not good. It's hers.

---

14. FINALE — The Livestream (pp.92-103)

a) [Record shop, day]
Lulu convinces the shop owner to let her do a small in-store event. Not branded. Not polished. She prints one poster: "A SONG FOR THREE BODIES. Free. Bring nothing."

b) [Jiro's apartment, day]
Jiro gets a text from Lulu. He reads it. Puts the phone down. Then picks it up again.

c) [Sara's room, day]
Sara sees the poster on Instagram — reposted by a mutual friend. She stares at it. The song title.

d) [Record shop, night — the event, pp.95-103]

The shop is small. Maybe 20 people. Some from the neighborhood. Some who saw the poster. Jiro is there, at the back. Sara is there, near the door, ready to leave.

Lulu speaks into the mic. No performance. No teacher voice.

Lulu: "I spent a long time recording other people's sounds because I didn't trust my own. This is the first song I've written where I can't blame anyone else for how it sounds."

She plays. The song is about three people. It's not sentimental. It's not resolved. It has the melody of "สุดท้ายแล้ว" buried in it — the same chord progression, slowed down, stripped of synths. A cover of a terrible song that became true.

During the song:
- Jiro watches. He's not smiling or crying. He's just present. The most honest he's been all film.
- Sara watches. She doesn't film it. She doesn't post it. She just… listens.
- The song ends. Applause. Lulu looks up. Sees both of them. No one moves toward anyone.

Lulu's voiceover, over the applause: "I named the final file WITHOUT-A-TITLE-01. The sound of a room full of people who don't know what happens next. I think it's the most honest thing I've ever recorded."

---

15. FINAL IMAGE (pp.104-105)
[Record shop, closing time — a different day]

The shop is empty. Jiro is there. He bought something — a CD. He walks out.

Sara is outside. She's bought a plant. The one from her failed content. It's still alive.

They see each other. Neither speaks. She holds up the plant. He nods. One nod meaning: "You kept it alive." She nods back. Meaning: "I'm trying."

They part — different directions.

Lulu, alone in the shop, arranging the orphaned CDs. She finds a new one — a blank CD-R. On the label, in her handwriting: "THE SONGS I HAVEN'T WRITTEN YET."

She holds it. Puts it in the player.

Nothing plays. The CD is blank.

She smiles.

Freeze frame. The pop song "สุดท้ายแล้ว" — In the End, After All — plays over the credits. This time, not ironic. Just the whole track. Its terrible synth. Its honest voice. All the way through.

APPENDIX: Strange Objects & Recurring Details

ROOM OBJECTS:
1. The ceiling fan that only works on speed 3 (throughout).
2. The landlady's RULES taped to Jiro's kitchen wall — we see them seven times across the film, sometimes in focus, sometimes blurry background.
3. The jar of fermented fish in the corner — nobody ever eats it. It's just there.
4. Sara's plant (the one from content Day 1).
5. The CD-R of "สุดท้ายแล้ว" — changes meaning across acts.

LULU'S SOUND FILE LABELS (in order of appearance):
- KREUNG-01 — ceiling fan on speed 3
- CITY-47 — the hum between Siam and Chit Lom
- BERLIN-12 — wet footsteps + laughing
- SARA-01 — return of a deleted signal
- UNDER-OVER-01 — rain on rust + heartbeat
- SARA-04 — the way she breathes before she lies
- HOTEL-12 — AC unit + silence after truth
- JIRO-09 — the sound of someone who trusts you
- LOVE-01 — three people not knowing what to do with their hands
- CITY-91 — the sound of nobody waiting at the end of the line
- WITHOUT-A-TITLE-01 — a room full of people who don't know what happens next

RECURRING POP SONG "สุดท้ายแล้ว" (In the End, After All):
- Play 1 (Act 1): Lulu finds it at the record shop. Plays it. Laughs at it. It's a joke, a curio.
- Play 2 (Act 2B midpoint): The triad sings it together badly, drunk, laughing. It becomes their inside thing.
- Play 3 (Act 3 finale): Lulu's song mirrors its chord progression. It's no longer a joke. It's the truth she was hiding behind irony.
- End credits: Full song, all the way through. Unironic. Earned.
